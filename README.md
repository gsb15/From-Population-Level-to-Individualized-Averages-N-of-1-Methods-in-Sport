Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) remain the gold standard for establishing causal effects and informing evidence-based practice. However, population-level averages may obscure meaningful individual variation, particularly in sport and exercise medicine, where training responses, recovery patterns, and injury risk can differ substantially between athletes. Our aim is to to describe the value of N-of-1 methodologies as a complementary analytic framework to traditional group-based approaches and demonstrate their application using real-world athlete monitoring data. A practical example was conducted using openly available Garmin running data from the Running Through project. Session-level measures of heart rate, speed, and distance were analyzed. N-of-1 methods provide a valuable complement to traditional group-based research by shifting the unit of inference from the population to the individual athlete. When combined with dense longitudinal monitoring data, these approaches can enhance personalized decision-making related to training, rehabilitation, and recovery. Integrating N-of-1 methodologies alongside conventional statistical models may strengthen the applicability of sport science evidence in real-world practice.
